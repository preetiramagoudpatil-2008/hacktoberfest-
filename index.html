ARCHIVAL PORTRAITS ACROSS INDIA

Voices that carry a place

Six artists from different regions show how a voice can carry a home, a lineage and a listening tradition across generations.

BEFORE 1941

HANGAL, KARNATAKA · HINDUSTANI VOCAL

Gangubai Hangal

Born in Hangal in 1913, Hangal became a major voice of the Kirana gharana and a defining figure in Karnataka's Hindustani music history.

View photograph · public domain ↗
1962

RON, KARNATAKA · HINDUSTANI VOCAL

Bhimsen Joshi

Born in Ron in 1922, Joshi brought the Kirana gharana to listeners across India through a celebrated career in Hindustani classical music.

British Institute of Recorded Sound · public domain ↗
1940S

GOA · HINDUSTANI VOCAL

Kesarbai Kerkar

Raised in Goa, Kerkar carried the khayal tradition with a refined phrase and a deeply rooted performance practice that is still remembered.

Learn more about Kesarbai Kerkar ↗
1950S

MADURAI, TAMIL NADU · CARNATIC VOCAL

M. S. Subbulakshmi

Born in Madurai, Subbulakshmi became one of the most influential Carnatic voices of the twentieth century, carrying the tradition into concert halls and homes.

Learn more about M. S. Subbulakshmi ↗
1970S

ANDHRA PRADESH · CARNATIC VOCAL

M. Balamuralikrishna

From Andhra Pradesh, Balamuralikrishna carried the grammar of Carnatic music with extraordinary vocal range and a deep attachment to place-bound tradition.

Learn more about M. Balamuralikrishna ↗
1980S

VARANASI, UTTAR PRADESH · SHEHNAI

Bismillah Khan

From Varanasi, Khan carried the sound of the shehnai into the public imagination while staying deeply tied to the sacred and musical geography of Banaras.

Learn more about Bismillah Khan ↗

Preservation begins

Music lives in more than a recording. It carries language, place, memory and the knowledge of its makers. Swarasetu is a starting point for documenting those connections with care.
